Paws & Claws: Australia's Love Affair with Pets

Exploring the animals Australians love, where they live, and the growing cost of companionship. There are about 31.6 million pets in 7.7 million homes, more pets than people.

Each square is 250,000 pets. There are 127 squares here. The 27.3 million people in Australia would fill about 109. Fish drive the count: at 12.1 million, there are more pet fish than pet dogs (7.4 million).

Source: Animal Medicines Australia (AMA), Pets in Australia 2025. Population figure: Australian Bureau of Statistics, as cited in the AMA report.

From tropical Queensland to coastal Victoria

Three local councils show how recorded pet registrations vary geographically. These are administrative records, not estimates of pet ownership. Townsville includes cat approvals and dog registrations (March 2026); Surf Coast includes registered records only; Golden Plains records have no registration-status filter. Different rules and reporting dates mean totals should not be treated as directly comparable ownership rates.

Sources: Townsville City Council animal registrations; Surf Coast Shire Registered Animals; Golden Plains Shire Animal Registrations. Source CSV summaries are included in the data folder. Boundaries: ABS Local Government Areas 2025 and Suburbs and Localities 2021 (GDA94). Counts reflect council registration/approval records, not all pet ownership. Unmatched suburb names are omitted from suburb-level maps; council totals should not be inferred from the map. Source dates and registration definitions differ.

Three in four homes now have a pet

Ownership dipped slightly before 2020, then jumped during the pandemic years. It kept climbing afterwards, so this was not just a lockdown fad.

Dogs and cats both gained ground since 2019. Dogs are in nearly half of all homes. Cats are in a third.

A dog costs about $860 a year more than a cat

An average dog-owning home spends $2,520 a year. A cat-owning home spends $1,656. Food is the biggest bill for almost every pet.

Reptile owners are the exception: vet bills are their biggest cost.

Together, Australians now spend about a third more on pets than in 2022, and most of that growth comes from dogs.

How to read the table: darker means more money. Blank cells mean the survey did not ask about that item for that pet.

Cats are usually adopted. Dogs are usually bought.

Three in four cats came from a shelter, a stray or a friend. For dogs, it is fewer than half.

Dogs are ahead on vet visits, microchipping and recent vaccination. Cats are ahead on desexing.

Pets are family, and owners act like it

Almost every pet-parenting habit grew since 2022. Six in ten owners talk to their pets as if they understand. One in seven has installed a camera to check on them.

Love is growing, and so is the strain

Eight in ten owners report at least some difficulty, up from 79% in 2022 and 66% in 2019. Ongoing costs are now the top difficulty.

One in eight pet-owning homes needed financial help to pay a vet bill. About one in six dog and cat owners cut back on health care, such as skipping check-ups or vaccines.
